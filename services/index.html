What's the Difference Between Carbon Neutral and Net Zero?

Carbon neutral typically means balancing emissions through offsetting without necessarily reducing absolute emissions, whilst net zero requires deep emissions reductions with offsetting only used for unavoidable residual emissions. Net zero represents a more rigorous approach that prioritises actual emission reductions over offset purchases.

Net zero commitments should include interim targets, clear timelines for deep emission reductions, and transparent reporting on progress. We help organisations understand the implications of different commitment types and develop strategies that meet stakeholder expectations whilst maintaining commercial viability and scientific credibility.

What Are Science-Based Targets and Do I Need Them?

Science-Based Targets (SBTs) are emission reduction targets that align with climate science requirements for limiting global warming to 1.5°C. SBTs provide credible frameworks for emission reduction that satisfy stakeholder expectations whilst ensuring targets contribute meaningfully to global climate goals.

Whilst SBT commitment isn't mandatory, it's increasingly expected by investors, customers, and supply chain partners. SBTs provide clear frameworks for target setting, progress measurement, and stakeholder communication that support business development whilst demonstrating genuine climate leadership. We help organisations assess whether SBT commitment aligns with business strategy and develop implementation pathways.

How Do I Measure and Report Scope 3 Emissions?

Scope 3 emissions measurement requires systematic data collection across supply chains, travel, waste, and other indirect emission sources.
