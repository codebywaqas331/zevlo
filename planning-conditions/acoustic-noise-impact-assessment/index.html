How Assessments Work

We initially determine the state of the existing acoustic environment, undertake acoustic noise surveys to understand and capture any existing sources creating noise around or near the site. A sound meter is used to measure the actual noise levels from these sources.

Our Assessment Process

1Noise Survey Data Collection

In order to collect the noise survey data, we would typically record measurements during the day and at night. The measurement process requires the dominant sources to be measured. These include things like the weather, the local traffic noise and the amount of HGV's around the area.

2Noise Mapping & Analysis

Once the data is collected, we use it to build a noise map. The various colours on the colour shaded map highlight the varying degrees of noise levels on site. The map is also used to show sound levels at various specified receptor points and helps show how noise target levels for external areas can be achieved to BS8233.

3Building Recommendations

Once the sound level data has been collected and analysed, the values can help us make recommendations on what building elements to use. These can include recommendations on the types of glazing to be used in the construction, as well as ventilation to make sure that the desired internal noise levels can be achieved to discharge any planning and building regulations conditions.

Key Standards & Compliance

BS8233 Residential Development Acoustic Criteria

The BS8233 Residential Development Acoustic Standard was put in place to help improve the acoustic design of internal and external living spaces in a residential development. The aim of the standard is to check and take account of external noise factors such as aircraft, rail, road traffic noise and more and mitigate against these factors with acoustic design.

The BS8233 standard provides a structured framework for residential developers who are building a project close to known sources of noise. The guidance assists in acoustic design that may include features such as ventilation design, building orientation as well as façade treatments and external space design. Compliance with BS8233 helps builders discharge related planning conditions.

BS4142 Industrial & Commercial Noise Assessment

As the title suggests, the BS4142: 2014+A1:2019 methodology was put in place to determine the impact of commercial and industrial noise on residential receptors. The main objective of the standard was to measure background noise levels and characterise the source of the noise emissions. The BS4142 standard aims to establish the likelihood of noise complaints from noise sources.

Assessments to the BS4142 are normally required to discharge planning conditions related to noise from commercial, industrial and mixed use developments that may affect residential areas.
