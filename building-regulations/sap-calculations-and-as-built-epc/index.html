When SAP Calculations & As-Built EPC Is Required

SAP calculations are mandatory for most new residential buildings and extensions under Approved Document Part L of the Building Regulations. The Standard Assessment Procedure provides standardised methodology for evaluating energy performance, carbon emissions, and compliance with energy efficiency targets across different building types and construction approaches.

As-built EPCs are required upon completion to demonstrate actual energy performance achievement and provide essential information for building occupiers about energy costs, environmental impact, and improvement recommendations. These certificates form part of the compliance demonstration required for Building Control completion certificates and final approval.

Mandatory Requirements

New Dwellings

  • All new houses and flats
  • Conversions creating new dwellings
  • Extensions over 100m² or 25% increase
  • Material changes of use

Commercial Buildings

  • New non-domestic buildings
  • Major renovations over 1000m²
  • Extensions and alterations
  • Changes of use requiring assessment

Timing Requirements

Design-stage SAP calculations must be submitted with Building Regulations applications to demonstrate compliance before construction commences. As-built calculations and EPCs are required before occupation, with certificates valid for 10 years and mandatory for sales and lettings.

Standards & Methods — Approved Document Part L

Approved Document Part L establishes energy efficiency requirements through Target Emission Rate (TER) and Target Primary Energy Rate (TPER) compliance demonstation, alongside specific provisions for building fabric performance, heating system efficiency, and renewable energy integration. SAP methodology provides standardised calculation procedures that ensure consistent assessment across different building types and design approaches.

Part L1A — New Dwellings

Key Requirements

  • Target Emission Rate (TER) compliance
  • Target Primary Energy Rate (TPER) compliance
  • Fabric Energy Efficiency (FEE) limits
  • Building fabric performance standards

Assessment Methods

  • SAP 10.2 calculation methodology
  • Standardised heating patterns
  • Climate data integration
  • Carbon emission factors

Part L1B — Extensions and Renovations

Extensions and renovations follow simplified compliance routes based on extent of work, with consequential improvements required for larger projects. Assessment considers thermal bridging, air permeability, and heating system efficiency to ensure overall building performance improvement.

Compliance Routes

  • Elemental method for small extensions
  • Whole building SAP for larger works
  • Consequential improvement requirements

Performance Targets

  • U-value compliance for new elements
  • Air permeability achievement
  • Heating system efficiency

FAQs — SAP Calculations & As-Built EPC

When do I need to submit SAP calculations with my Building Regulations application?

Design stage SAP calculations must be submitted with your initial Building Regulations application for all new dwellings and applicable extensions. These demonstrate compliance with Part L energy requirements before construction commences.

What happens if my as-built performance doesn't match the design predictions?

Minor variations are normal due to construction tolerances and material substitutions. Significant performance gaps may require remedial measures or alternative compliance demonstration. We work with you to identify solutions that maintain Building Regulations compliance.

Do I need separate SAP calculations for each dwelling in a development?

Yes, each dwelling requires individual SAP assessment and EPC, though similar dwelling types can use standardised calculations with appropriate adjustments for orientation, shading, and specific features. This ensures accurate performance assessment for each property.

How long are EPCs valid and when do they need renewal?

EPCs are valid for 10 years from issue date and are required for all property sales and lettings. New EPCs may be needed earlier if significant building improvements are made or if energy performance changes substantially.

Can SAP calculations help optimise my building design for energy efficiency?

Yes, SAP modelling during design development enables performance optimisation through fabric specification, heating system selection, and renewable energy integration. We provide recommendations that balance compliance, cost, and performance to achieve optimal building design.
